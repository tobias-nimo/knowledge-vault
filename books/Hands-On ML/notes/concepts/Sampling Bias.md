# Sampling Bias
Sampling bias occurs when the sample used to train or evaluate a model is **not representative of the overall population**. As a result, patterns learned from the sample may **not generalize well** to real-world data.

In machine learning, sampling bias can cause the training set, test set, or both to contain different proportions of important groups than those present in the full dataset. This leads to unreliable model evaluation and potentially poor performance in production.

## Famous Examples
- **1936 U.S. Presidential Election Poll**: The magazine _Literary Digest_ predicted that Alf Landon would defeat Franklin D. Roosevelt after surveying millions of people. However, the sample was heavily biased toward wealthier Americans (drawn from telephone directories and automobile registrations), making it unrepresentative of the voting population. Roosevelt won by a landslide.
- **Medical Studies Based Only on Hospital Patients**: Early medical research often relied primarily on hospitalized patients. Since hospitalized individuals tend to have more severe conditions than the general population, conclusions drawn from these samples sometimes failed to generalize to the broader public.
